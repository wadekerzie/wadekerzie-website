2 of 5 · My read

How Lumen buys

At Lumen, every dollar walks through the same door. Know the door and you know how to size the deal.

The committee

One docket, once a month

All spend, operating spend included, goes through a monthly capital governance committee. Inside, it is just "cap gov." It wants a hard payback measured in months, not years.

I have watched this motion for years, from the selling side. I do not expect it to change much.

The clock

A slip costs a month

A proposal that misses the docket, or shows up without the payback story the committee expects, waits for the next one. There is no in-between meeting.

So the calendar is part of the deal. Timing a proposal is as much of the work as writing it.

The labor gap

Fewer people, same network

Lumen cut staff. The network still has to be built and maintained. So Lumen buys outside crews in short engagements rather than adding headcount.

That is work WWT already sells.

What it means for how we sell

Every proposal is sized and timed to clear the monthly docket, with payback in months. Short, repeatable engagements that each stand on their own beat one large ask that sits in committee.

Two paths

One mission, two paths, worked at the same time. Neither goes first. One is WWT's core services pointed at what Lumen has to do now. The other is a revenue stream Lumen does not have yet.

Path one

WWT's core services, pointed at Lumen

Nothing has to be invented to start. WWT's existing service portfolio already covers what Lumen has to do with fewer people. The specific needs are discovered in the first weeks, with the groups that own the work, not guessed from the outside.

  • Strategic resourcing and deployment. Staffing, field install, and engineer-furnish-install (EF&I). The direct answer to the labor gap after the staff cuts.
  • Consulting and engineering. Design and architecture help for a network being rebuilt for the AI economy.
  • ATC lab services and the AI Proving Ground. Test and prove it before the capital is committed. That is the payback story cap gov wants to see.
  • Supply chain and integration. Warehousing, staging, kitting, integration centers and logistics, for a network refresh happening at scale.
  • Managed and lifecycle services. Running and maintaining what gets built, in engagements sized to the docket.
Path two

GPU as a Service

Lumen's footprint, central offices and similar space, has square footage that produces no revenue today. GPU as a Service turns that space into a new revenue stream. It is where WWT's NVIDIA partnership and Lumen's network meet.

  • What it is. Real estate repurposing. Space Lumen already owns, compute designed with WWT's NVIDIA partnership and proven in the AI Proving Ground first, connected by the network Lumen is already building.
  • Why it fits cap gov. Square footage that goes from idle to earning is a payback story the committee understands.
  • Kept conceptual on purpose. Which sites, which customers, and what size come out of the conversations inside Lumen, not from a slide.

Why both at once

Both paths start with the same people on the same calendar. Path one is work Lumen needs now. Path two is revenue Lumen does not have yet. Each one earns the right to talk about the other.
